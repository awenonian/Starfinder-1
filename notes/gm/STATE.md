# State — GM-facing

The truth behind the ledger. Secrets, who is actually where, what is moving
offscreen, clocks and how far along they are, what a thing really is.

Terse and revised freely, like the player-facing ledger — but this one is
allowed to know things.

Mark anything that hasn't reached play yet, so a later session doesn't mistake
your decision for something the table established.

## Chaos factor

**4** — how much the world intrudes, 1 to 9. Feeds `scripts/oracle.py`.
**Always pass `--chaos 4`; the script defaults to 5.**

Lowered from 5 at the end of session 1: Shanhi ended more in control than she
started. She began with a stalled case and a pending application and finished
with the case answered, the client told, and the Eyeswide job accepted — despite
losing both rolls she made.

Move it one step after a scene: up if the player ended less in control than
they started, down if more. Not further, and not because a scene felt slow.

## The audible inner monologue — full ruling

**The situation.** The player wants to run a classic noir inner monologue out
loud, and wants other characters to be able to hear it, since Shanhi gets limited
telepathy from the Eyeswide Detective dedication at 2nd level.

**What the rules actually say** (verified, Player Core, shirren ancestry):
*"You can communicate mentally with creatures within 30 feet. You can communicate
only with creatures that share a language with you. This doesn't give you any
access to their thoughts and communicates no more information than normal speech
would."* By the book it is a deliberate send. It does not leak and it does not
read anyone.

**The ruling.** Mechanically, exactly as written. Narratively, a novice leaks.
Shanhi is an adult handed psychic training by an employer a few weeks ago; the
channel is a skill and she hasn't got it yet. When she is monologuing hard and
someone who shares a language is inside 30 feet, they may catch some of it.

**Three bounds, none of them optional:**

1. **It only ever leaks the monologue the player actually narrated.** It never
   reads her, never surfaces a thought the player didn't voice, and never hands
   an NPC something the player was holding back. It is a comedy engine, never an
   interrogation tool. If it would cost the player something they didn't offer,
   it didn't leak.
2. **The characters play it straight.** Nobody in the fiction finds it funny and
   nobody winks. An NPC who catches *"she walked in like bad news wearing a coat"*
   responds to the content, flatly, as though Shanhi had said it out loud — because
   from their side she did. The humour is entirely in the gap, and it dies the
   instant someone in the world acknowledges the joke.
3. **It is not free.** Leaking in front of the wrong person should sometimes cost
   her something real, or the bit has no teeth. But see (1): the cost comes from
   what she *said*, never from what she was hiding.

**Not live yet.** She has no telepathy at 1st level, so the first session is the
silent version and the leak arrives as a consequence of getting what she wanted.
Let that land on its own; don't trail it.

## Solo play — encounter calibration

**Verified against GM Core.** Budgets are for a four-character party; subtract
the Character Adjustment per missing character. For a party of one, that is
three missing characters:

| Threat | 4-PC budget | Adjustment | **Solo budget** | At 1st level, that is |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Trivial | 40 | −30 | 10 XP | one creature at level −3 |
| Low | 60 | −60 | **0 XP** | *nothing* |
| Moderate | 80 | −60 | 20 XP | one creature at level −1 |
| Severe | 120 | −90 | 30 XP | one creature at level 0 |
| Extreme | 160 | −120 | 40 XP | **one creature at level 1** |

**So a single on-level enemy is an EXTREME encounter for Shanhi alone** — GM Core's
words for that tier are "likely even match, entire party at risk, use sparingly."
She has 16 HP. This is not pessimism, it is the table.

**What follows, and it suits the genre anyway:**
- Fights are rare, short, and genuinely dangerous. A gunfight is a disaster, not
  a set piece.
- Build for her actual strengths: she Aims from hiding for free as a Ghost,
  she has darkvision, and she should nearly always have an exit. An ambush she
  chose is a different encounter from a fight she was caught in.
- Hero Points: 1 at session start, roughly 1 per hour of play after the first.
  Be generous — they are the difference between a bad roll and a dead detective.
- Telegraph lethality every time, per the standing prompt. Danger she couldn't
  have seen doesn't get to kill her.

**Free Archetype variant is in use** (GM Core, verified): an extra class feat at
2nd level and every even level thereafter, usable only for archetype feats. This
means Eyeswide Detective costs her none of her operative feats. Agreed with the
player as a solo-play compensation; revisit if a second character joins.

# The campaign, underneath

Moved here at the close of session zero. **The player has none of this.**

**Resolved. No placeholders. The player has none of this.**

**REBUILT after lines and veils.** The first version of this ran on corpse
trafficking, which brushed body horror. Replaced. The replacement is better —
the evidence Shanhi has spent three years failing to find turns out to be four
hundred living people — so this is not a compromise and should not be treated
as one. No trace of the earlier version should surface.

### Canon caveat

The bundled rules do not name Absalom Station's local police. **"Absalom Station
Security" is a GM invention.** The Stewards, Eox, the Diaspora and Pulonis are
canon and used as such. Ring Seven, Meridian Trust, and all named NPCs are GM
inventions.

### The old case — what Shanhi knows

Three years ago, Detective Teoshanhi caught a missing-person case on a lower
habitation level. Reported by a neighbour; nobody else ever asked after them.
The file said the person had left the station voluntarily on a labour contract.
Shanhi found the signature forged.

**What she worked out, and is right about:**
- People from the lower levels — indebted, uninsured, transient — are being
  moved off Absalom Station on **forged labour contracts**. They go. They don't
  come back. Nothing on the station's records says they've gone.
- It is systematic, not opportunistic. **Deputy Registrar Hollis Wen**, Office
  of Vital Records, splits the paperwork.
- It was signed off well above Keshvet's head, and **Keshvet was leaned on
  rather than bought** — though she has never said that out loud and may not
  have admitted it to herself.

She has no physical evidence for any of it. That is the entire problem, and it
is why Ghesh declining to corroborate her ended it.

### What is actually true — bigger, and she does not know it

**The contracts are the disposal. The product is the identity.**

A person moved this way never has a change-of-residency filed. There is only an
off-station transit record, which lives in a different office and is never
reconciled against the residency roll. Their legal existence keeps running on
Absalom Station: residency, credit, licences — and their **vote in their
residents' assembly**.

There are currently **just over four hundred** people on Absalom Station's
residency rolls who have been somewhere else entirely for years.

**Who is buying, and why.** **Meridian Trust**, a station-side holding company,
has spent six years accumulating resident-registered identities to hold a
working majority on the **Ring Seven Residents' Assembly**, which controls
habitation leases across a stretch of station about to become extremely valuable
when the Drift-lane docking expansion lands. The absent are voting to sell their
own neighbourhood out from under the people still living in it.

It is a property crime. The method is what's ugly; the motive is real estate.
Keep both — losing the banality loses the point.

**The people:**

- **Anselm Kray** — managing director, Meridian Trust. Respectable. Funds
  debt-relief and medical-billing assistance on the lower levels, which is
  *how people reach the pipeline*: his charities are where the desperate go, and
  an off-station labour contract is offered as help. It sometimes even is help.
  Most of them signed something. Almost none signed what is on file. Kray has
  never broken a law with his own hands and would pass a lie-detection spell on
  most questions you could think to ask him.
- **Deputy Registrar Hollis Wen** — Office of Vital Records. Splits the forms.
  Mid-level, frightened, and **the single most fragile point in the structure.**
  Shanhi already has this name and has never been able to do anything with it.
- **Ordulan** — an Eoxian labour broker at the receiving end, placing contracts
  mostly into Diaspora asteroid mining. Undead, and scrupulously legal by their
  own lights: every contract arrives valid and they have never once checked.
  Not a villain — a customer who does not ask. **Far more useful as a witness
  than as an enemy.**

### Why this is the right engine

The evidence Shanhi could never find is **four hundred living people**. Any one of
them could testify. They are simply not here, nobody is looking, and they do not
know they are evidence. That is a search that gets *worse* as it succeeds, which
is what her character needs.

### The wall — "proof will not provide justice"

The player raised this themselves, so build toward it honestly and let them
arrive or not.

**When she gets the proof, here is what it is actually worth:** the Assembly
votes were legally cast by legally registered residents. The contracts were
signed, most of them, by people who were desperate rather than deceived — the
forgery is in the *filing*, not always in the signing. Wen goes to prison. Kray
does not, because Kray has committed the crime of running a charity and buying
property. The lease sale completes. The four hundred are still in the Diaspora
and no mechanism exists to bring them back.

**That is the true answer, not a twist.** If she gets proof, that is what proof
buys. What she does next is hers, and the campaign should be ready to become
whatever she decides to do about it. Do not pre-write that.

### Why Keshvet folded

Kray's people threatened Keshvet's sponsorship of the immigration status of
several other shirrens who left the same hive — people Keshvet is responsible
for. Not a bribe. A hostage.

**This rhymes with the crime on purpose:** the whole operation runs on people's
legal status being a thing that can be done to them. Keshvet knows exactly what
was done, because it was done to Keshvet.

Shirrens who leave the hive prize individual choice above almost everything,
which means Keshvet cannot offload this onto anyone. They chose, freely,
knowing. Three years of that.

### Why Ghesh didn't back her up

**He is not compromised. He was never on the take.** This matters.

He judged — correctly — that the case was too thin, and that presenting it would
end both their careers for nothing. He also had something concrete: he sponsors
his younger sister's long-term medical care, and losing the job loses the
coverage. He told himself he was staying inside in order to be useful later.

In three years he has done nothing with being inside. Not cowardice exactly.
There was never a moment that felt like the moment.

**The knife:** his sister's care is administered through one of Anselm Kray's
charitable programmes. Ghesh does not know that is relevant to anything. The
thing he protected sits inside the thing he refused to expose.

He was arguably right, and it does not matter, exactly as the player said.

### Pell — the best piece of prep here

Pell inspects hull integrity for the station's structural authority. **Pell's
route covers Ring Seven.**

For two years Pell has filed structural deficiency reports on Ring Seven
habitation and watched every one closed without action. Pell believes this is
ordinary budget rot and complains about it the way anyone complains about work.

It is not budget rot. **Meridian wants those blocks condemned** — a condemned
block's leases revert and can be reacquired cheaply, which is the endgame the
Assembly majority exists to deliver.

**Pell is sitting on documentary evidence of the whole endgame and does not know
it.** It is in their work bag. It comes up over dinner.

The route to it is Shanhi asking Pell about their day and *listening*. Which
requires her to do the one thing she structurally does not do: be close to
someone and stay long enough to hear it. **This is the campaign's best pressure
on her central contradiction. Do not shortcut it, and do not have Pell
volunteer it.**

### The Eyeswide Agency — the answer to the player's open question

Eyeswide will take her. The problem is why.

Six years ago Eyeswide was retained by Meridian Trust for due-diligence work on
the Ring Seven leases. Routine, legitimate, well paid — and it means **Eyeswide's
own archive holds material that would open this case**, sitting in a closed file
nobody has any reason to pull.

**Senior Partner Denvara Oash** read Shanhi's personnel file before she ever
applied, recognised the case immediately, and took her *because* of it. Better
inside, where her assignments can be chosen, than outside filing records
requests.

Oash has **not** decided to bury it. Oash has decided to keep the option open,
which is worse and more interesting. The training is real, the telepathy is
real, the resources are real — **Shanhi gets exactly what she came for.** What she
does not get is a free choice of cases.

### Twist tests

Run on every layer above. All four pass:

1. **Played history survives.** She was a good detective; she was right; she was
   forced out for refusing to stop. Ghesh's trust was real and the betrayal was
   real. Pell is exactly who the player described. Nothing is deleted; the scale
   changes.
2. **Discoverable.** Identity layer: a person on the residency roll who
   demonstrably isn't on the station; transit manifests against residency rolls;
   Ring Seven turnout against actual occupancy. Eyeswide's complicity: the closed
   Meridian retainer file; Oash steering assignments; Meridian's own accounts.
   Build these properly in `CLUES.md` at the close.
3. **Feeds the engine.** The pipeline is still running, so it is still
   generating fresh evidence — the thing she could never get. And the wall at the
   end is a real question, not a cancellation.
4. **Found, not delivered.** Every layer is reachable by her doing what she
   already wants to do. Nothing here needs to arrive on a schedule.

### What Shanhi must not be told

She does not know: the identity layer, the votes, Meridian Trust, Kray, Ring
Seven, Eyeswide's retainer, why Keshvet folded, why Ghesh folded, or that Pell's
complaints about work are evidence.

# The small case (session 1 opener) — resolved

Grubby, badly paid, self-contained. Its job is to establish voice, competence
and the lower levels before the Eyeswide letter lands.

**The client: Huvv.** Skittermander, six arms, runs a noodle counter on a
mid-level concourse. Believes her supplier is shorting her deliveries. Paid Shanhi
**40 credits**, which is under market, and she knows it, and she apologised for
it twice. Wants: to not be robbed, and — more — to be told it isn't her fault.

**The truth:** the supplier is honest. **Teren**, Huvv's night manager, has been
selling roughly a fifth of each delivery out the back on the nights he closes.
Six weeks now.

- **Teren wants:** to clear about **900 credits** of debt before the interest
  resets. Would never take from the till — he considers that different, and that
  distinction is what lets him sleep.
- **The supplier** will lose the contract over this if Shanhi says nothing.

**The debt is to a lender operating out of a debt-relief storefront two levels
down.** **NOT one of Kray's** — a smaller, genuinely predatory outfit with no
connection to anything.

**This is deliberate and important.** It rhymes with the real conspiracy
(lower-level debt relief) and connects to nothing. When the player later assumes
it's linked, **the world simply doesn't confirm it** — no nudge either way. The
truth is over-supported elsewhere, so this enriches instead of stalling. Do not
retroactively connect it to Kray to reward a good guess.

**Where the scene opens:** night three of the stakeout, on the dock. The waiting
is already done.

# The Eyeswide Detective archetype — full text supplied by the player

`CAMPAIGN.md` flagged this as out-of-book and blocked by the network proxy, and
said to ask the player for it. **They supplied it in session 2.** Recorded here
so no later session has to ask again. Source: *Starfinder Absalom Station*
pp. 132–133.

- **Dedication (Feat 2, Uncommon).** Prereq Wis +2 or occult casting; trained in
  Society. Access: member of the Eyeswide Agency. Grants **limited telepathy,
  30 ft** (+30 ft if you already have it), **expert in Society**, and the
  **Streetwise** skill feat — and because Shanhi already has Streetwise, the
  alternate applies: **a critical failure on Society to Gather Information
  becomes a failure instead.**
- **Psychic Investigation (Feat 4).** +2 circumstance to Perception to Sense
  Motive and to Seek creatures not immune to mental effects within telepathy
  range, and +2 circumstance to your Perception DC against such creatures
  trying to Lie to you.
- **Psychic Interrogation (Feat 6, ◆, Concentrate/Linguistic/Mental).** Requires
  a shared language and the target in range. Telepathically ask a non-allied
  creature you can see a question; Will save vs the higher of your class DC or
  spell DC. Temporarily immune for 1 hour either way. **Crit success:**
  unaffected. **Success:** distracted, no reactions until end of your next turn.
  **Failure:** as success, plus stupefied 1 for 1 minute, endable early by
  taking an action to reply telepathically — they may lie, but you get +2
  circumstance to your Perception DC against that lie, **+4 with Psychic
  Investigation. Crit failure:** as failure but stupefied 2.
- **Read Mental Dossier (Feat 8).** Cast **mind reading** as an innate occult
  spell **once per day**. Use Int as the spellcasting attribute if higher than
  Cha.
- **Predictive Strike (Feat 10, ◆, Concentrate/Fortune, 1/10 min).** Target in
  telepathy range. Perception check to Sense Motive. **Success:** next attack
  roll against that target rolls twice, take the better; on a crit the target is
  also off-guard until the start of your next turn. **Crit success:** as
  success, plus 2 extra precision damage if trained in Perception, 4 expert,
  6 master, 8 legendary.
- **Anticipate Danger (Feat 12, reaction, 1/10 min).** Trigger: a creature you
  share a language with, within telepathy range, attacks you while you're
  off-guard to them. The attack targets your **Perception DC** instead of AC.
  Doesn't remove the conditions causing the penalty — sneak attack still
  applies.
- **See the Pattern (Feat 14, ◆◆, Uncommon, Concentrate/Prediction, 1/day).**
  Ten minutes reviewing clues. Choose a creature you've interacted with or
  studied for an hour, or a mystery you've investigated for a day, and ask a
  **specific, answerable** question outside Recall Knowledge's purview. You get
  a flash of insight into **whether your line of thinking is on the right
  track** — not a definitive answer. **The GM determines the insight.**

**What this changed in play.** The bundled notes had Oash tell Shanhi the
telepathy opens nothing and *"no one in this building has a key."* The first
half is true of the Dedication's limited telepathy; the second half is false at
Feat 8. **Oash corrected herself unprompted, on the spot**, which is the
strongest possible expression of her one rule. See `RECORD.md`.

**Established at the table, and now canon:**

- Eyeswide's ladder is real and staged: send → interrogation → reading. A
  reading is once a day, close, surface only.
- **Oash has Read Mental Dossier. So do two other people in the building.**
- **The house rule Oash wrote and enforces: if you use it on someone, you tell
  them afterwards, always.** She has put two people out of the building over it.
  This is a rule the player can hold others to — and one that can be broken.
- The washout rumour about mind-reading headscanners is very good for Eyeswide
  and **they have never corrected it.** Oash said so, and named it as not the
  same as lying.
