# Talia Vandermeer

Human, 71. Retired ship-fitter — forty years in the Cantrell yards on Ring
Three. Lives at **6-114**, a residential terrace on Ring Three Level Six, with a
live-in carer. Owns the flat outright. Slow on her feet. Not slow anywhere else.
Does the crossword in the transit bulletin in about nine minutes.

The card in her doorframe, hand-lettered, older than the paint around it:
**NO CANVASSERS, NO CHARITY, NO EXCEPTIONS.**

## VOICE

Verbatim, session 2, all of it through a closed or closing door — **she has not
yet been seen on-page:**

- "Who is it, Odett?"
- "The terrace association doesn't knock, Odett."
- "If you've got business with me, put it in writing. I'm not deaf, I'm not senile, and I don't open my door."
- "And you can stop standing in the hall, Odett."

Dry, unhurried, pitched to carry exactly as far as it needs to and no further.
Does not raise her voice and does not need to. Uses people's names as
punctuation.

**Can't say:** an explanation of herself. Not out of shame — she settled this
with herself decades ago and considers it nobody's business. Asked directly and
respectfully she says a true thing that is not the answer: *"It's my money and
it goes where I send it."*

## INTENTION

- **What she's for:** the person the fact is about, who is not the person who
  gets to choose. Shan's whole method — hand over the finding, refuse to
  advocate — breaks on this woman, because handing Corin the finding hands him
  Talia.
- **What she wants:** to be left to run her own affairs, as she has for fifty
  years. She will not be *managed*, and being managed is the one thing that
  reliably moves her.
- **What she'd never do:** open her door to a stranger, or let anyone see her
  decide anything. She'll answer a letter. She will not answer a doorstep.

## The truth — resolved before session 2, unchanged

Talia sends the sixty credits herself and has done for **thirty-eight years.**

A scaffold gave way in the fitting yards and a man named **Bevin Oduya** died.
The rigging call was hers. The finding was material fatigue; nobody was
disciplined. She has paid a monthly sum to Oduya's daughter ever since, through
a remittance house, and has never told the daughter, or her son, or anyone but
Odett.

- **Nera Oduya**, the daughter, works freight scheduling two rings over and has
  assumed for years that the payments are a pension administration error she'd
  be a fool to query.
- She lives on the remaining thirty-four credits a month. She has lived thin for
  thirty-eight years and considers the arithmetic closed.

## Knows, believes, lies

- **Knows:** all of it. Where the money goes, why, and that her son has been
  looking at her account.
- **Believes:** that it is nobody's business. Not a delusion — a position.
- **Lies about:** nothing. She declines, and she declines by changing the
  subject to something true.
- **Won't say:** Oduya's name, to anybody, ever, unprompted.

## Where she stands at the end of session 2

Odett lied to her, on the doorstep, about who was calling — *"Nobody, love.
Terrace association."* Talia caught it inside four seconds and said so out loud
in front of the caller. Then Shan knocked again, which told her the caller was
not the terrace association, and she used that on Odett immediately.

**She has told Shan to put it in writing.** That is a genuine channel and she
will read what arrives, because she reads everything. She did not come to the
door and will not.

**She now knows somebody is asking about her money.** She has not been told who
sent them and has not asked Shan. Unopposed, she asks Odett that evening whether
Corin sent someone, gets an answer she doesn't believe, and **says nothing and
waits.** She does not change the standing order — changing it would be admitting
something, and she is not going to.
