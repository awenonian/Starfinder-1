# Vey Torrance

Human, sixties. Retired detective, Absalom Station Security, twenty-six years.
Pensioned out three years ago — her spine gave up before she did. Walks with a
stick. Drinks at the Loading Gate, the unmarked bar under the old Fourth
Precinct, with her back to the wall.

Knew Shanhi on the force. Was not her partner and was not her friend. Introduced
in session 2 as Shanhi's route to Denvara Oash.

## VOICE

Verbatim, session 2, the Loading Gate:

- "Nobody comes down here who doesn't want something, and the ones who don't want anything are dead."
- "It *was* accidental. That's the point of the story."
- "She was standing outside the tape with her hands where I could see them without being asked, which nobody does."
- "There is not one false word in it. Not one. I went through it line by line looking for the place she lied to me and it isn't there."
- "Somebody wanted you out of the building. Nobody wanted you *ruined*."

Flat, dry, unsentimental. Tells a story she has clearly told before and enjoys
the shape of it — holds up fingers, does it slowly. Delivers the kind thing and
the cruel thing in exactly the same tone, without a shift, because she does not
sort them into different categories.

**Can't say:** that she misses it. Vey will not admit, at any price, that being
asked a professional question is the best thing that has happened to her this
month. She will keep answering and she will never say why.

## INTENTION

- **What she's for:** the retired-detective vector. A route to institutional
  memory that Shanhi cannot get from records and will not get from anyone still
  serving. Also the one person who will say a hard true thing to Shanhi's face
  without wanting anything back for it.
- **What she wants:** to still be a detective. She is not one. Nobody asks her
  anything any more. That is the whole engine — **she talks because she is
  asked.**
- **What she'd never do:** soften something to be kind, or ask Shanhi how she's
  doing. She'd also never carry a message to anyone still on the force; she is
  finished with that building and it is finished with her.
- **Does not solve problems.** She has memory and opinions and no reach. She
  cannot pull a file, cannot make a call that lands, and will say so.

## POSITIONS

None taken yet, in the sense of committing to something costly. She did
volunteer, unprompted and to Shanhi's face, that Shanhi's dismissal was
suspiciously gentle — see below. That was an observation, not an alliance.

## Knows, believes, lies

- **Knows:** twenty-six years of Station Security's institutional memory, at
  detective level. Case-by-case, not structural. She was never near the old
  case and has no idea Wen exists.
- **Believes:** that everybody in the Sarn story was right, and that this is the
  disturbing part. Correct.
- **Lies about:** nothing. Not a liar; too tired for it.
- **Won't say:** that she misses the job.

## The Sarn story — delivered in full, session 2

**Established at the table.** Roughly four years ago, a cargo transfer
supervisor named **Aldo Sarn** died at a Ring Four transfer berth, going into
the gap during a coupling. It was genuinely an accident and Station Security
knew it by lunchtime. The carrier's insurer sent Eyeswide anyway; Eyeswide sent
**Denvara Oash**.

Oash asked Vey's permission to sit with the widow, and waited for it. Two hours.
She came out and handed Security three things they did not have and were not
owed:

1. The berth had run the coupling manual for eleven days, because the interlock
   kept faulting.
2. The fault log was being cleared nightly by a man not authorised to clear it.
3. Sarn had been doing the walk-out himself, instead of sending the junior,
   every night for a month.

All true. All checked out. Case closed in a week.

**What she did not say:** that Sarn had been walking about forty credits a night
out of that berth in his own coat pockets — which is why he did the walk-out
himself, which is why he was standing where he was standing. Oash had it inside
the first half hour. It was not the insurer's question.

Vey turned up the skimming eleven months later on an unrelated matter, went back
and pulled the Eyeswide transcript — handed over the counter without a form —
and read it line by line. **Not one false word in it.** Oash never said Sarn was
clean and never said that was everything.

The insurer paid out. Security closed it accidental. It *was* accidental.
Everybody in the story was right.

**This is Oash's whole method, delivered as an anecdote rather than a warning,
by someone who admires her.** Clue D3 in `notes/gm/CLUES.md` — every evasion
Oash makes is a true statement — now has a worked example in the player's hands
*before* they meet her. Do not point at it again.
