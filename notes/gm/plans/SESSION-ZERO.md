### Canon caveat

The bundled Player Core does not name Absalom Station's local police force.
**"Absalom Station Security" is a GM invention**, not verified canon. The
Stewards (Pact Worlds mutual defence force) and Eox (member world of intelligent
undead) *are* canon and are used as such.

### The old case — what Shan knows

Roughly three years ago, Detective Teoshanhi of Absalom Station Security caught
a death on a lower habitation level: an overdose, unremarkable. The body was
processed and shipped off-station before she had closed the file. She pulled
the paperwork and found the consent forms forged.

**What she worked out, and is right about:**
- There is a standing pipeline moving unclaimed dead off Absalom Station to
  **Eox** on forged next-of-kin consent. The bodies are people nobody asks
  after — the lower levels, the uninsured, the transient.
- The forgery is systematic, not opportunistic. Someone is splitting the
  paperwork deliberately.
- She has a name at the clerical end: **Deputy Registrar Hollis Wen**, Office
  of Vital Records. She could never prove Wen did anything, and never learned
  who Wen answers to.
- It was signed off well above her lieutenant's head, and her lieutenant was
  leaned on rather than bought.

She has no physical evidence for any of it. That is the whole problem.

### What is actually true — bigger, and she does not know it

**The corpses are the disposal end. The product is the paperwork.**

A person processed this way is legally *not dead*. The death certificate is
never filed; only a transit consent, which is a different form in a different
office, and the two are never reconciled. The person's legal existence keeps
running: station residency, citizenship, licences, credit, and — the point —
their **vote on their residents' assembly**.

There are currently **just over four hundred** people on Absalom Station's rolls
who are alive on paper and were shipped to Eox as freight.

**Who is buying, and why:** **Meridian Trust**, a station-side holding company,
has spent six years accumulating resident-registered identities to hold a
working majority on the **Ring Seven Residents' Assembly**, which controls
habitation leases across a stretch of the station about to become very valuable
when the Drift-lane docking expansion lands. The dead are voting to sell their
own neighbourhood out from under the living ones.

It is, at bottom, a property crime. The horror is the method; the motive is real
estate. Keep both.

**The people:**

- **Anselm Kray** — managing director of Meridian Trust. Respectable. A
  philanthropist who funds free clinics on the lower levels, which is *how the
  bodies reach the pipeline*: his clinics are where the poor die. He does not
  think of himself as a murderer and is technically correct. He kills nobody.
  He simply owns the places where dying already happens and has monetised the
  paperwork afterwards.
- **Deputy Registrar Hollis Wen** — Office of Vital Records. Does the actual
  form-splitting. Mid-level, frightened, load-bearing, and the single most
  fragile point in the whole structure. Shan already has this name.
- **Ordulan** — an Eoxian freight broker on the receiving end. Undead, and
  scrupulously legal by their own lights: every shipment arrives with valid
  consent documentation and they have never once checked whether it is real.
  Not a villain. A customer who does not ask. More useful as a witness than as
  an enemy.

### Why the lieutenant stopped her

**Lieutenant Keshvet**, a shirren, was Shan's supervisor and told her to drop
it. Keshvet was not bought. Kray's people threatened Keshvet's sponsorship of
the immigration status of several other shirrens who left the same hive —
people Keshvet is responsible for.

Keshvet folded for a reason a decent person might fold for, and has had three
years to live with it. **Shirrens who leave the hive prize individual choice
above nearly everything**, which means Keshvet cannot offload this onto anyone.
They chose. Freely. Knowing.

Keshvet is still on the force, still senior, and has not been able to look at
Shan since. **This is the most useful NPC in the setup** and should not be spent
early.

### The Eyeswide Agency's angle — the answer to the player's open question

Eyeswide will take her. The problem is why.

Six years ago Eyeswide was retained by Meridian Trust for due-diligence work on
the Ring Seven leases. Routine, legitimate, well-paid — and it means **Eyeswide's
own archive contains material that would open this case**, sitting in a closed
file nobody has reason to pull.

**Senior Partner Denvara Oash** (lashunta) handles intake. Oash read Shan's
personnel file before she ever applied, recognised the case immediately, and
took her *because* of it. Better inside, where her assignments can be chosen,
than outside filing records requests.

Oash has **not** decided to bury it. Oash has decided to keep the option open,
which is worse and more interesting. The training is real, the telepathy is
real, the resources are real — Shan gets exactly what she came for. What she
does not get is a free choice of cases.

### Twist tests — run before committing

1. **Does the played history survive?** Yes. She really was a good detective,
   really was right, really was forced out for refusing to stop. The scale
   changes; nothing she did is deleted.
2. **Is it discoverable?** Yes, three independent routes to the identity layer:
   a dead person's credentials in current use; Eoxian freight manifests against
   unfiled death certificates; the Assembly's voting roll versus its residency
   roll. Three routes to Eyeswide's complicity: the closed Meridian retainer
   file; Oash steering her assignments; Meridian's own accounts. Build these
   properly in `CLUES.md` at the close.
3. **Does it feed the engine or eat it?** Feeds it. The pipeline is *still
   running*, which means it is still generating fresh evidence — the thing she
   could never get. The search gets worse, not finished.
4. **Do they find it, or does it arrive?** She finds it by doing precisely what
   she already wants to do. Nothing here needs delivering on a schedule.

### What Shan must not be told

She is right about the bodies. She is right that it goes high. She is right
about Keshvet being leaned on rather than bought — though she has never said so
out loud and may not have admitted it to herself.

She does not know: the identity layer, the voting, Meridian Trust, Kray,
Ring Seven, or anything about Eyeswide's retainer.
