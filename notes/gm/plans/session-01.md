# Session 1 — plan

**Prepped at the close of session zero. Nothing here has happened.**

Read `notes/gm/STATE.md` (parties, clocks, and the underneath) and
`notes/gm/CLUES.md` before running this. This file is only the opening.

## What this session has to do

1. **Establish the voice.** The noir monologue, the register, the tone. She has
   **no telepathy yet** — the leak arrives with the dedication at 2nd level, so
   session one is the silent version and that is deliberate. Don't trail it.
2. **Show her competent.** Before anything costs her, she should get to be good
   at this in front of the player.
3. **Land the Eyeswide acceptance**, at whatever point in the session it falls.
   She gets in. That is decided; what she doesn't know is why.
4. **Put at least one thing in front of her that she cannot leave alone.**

## Three places it could open — commit to none

Pick when the session starts, or let the player's first move pick. If none fits,
build a fourth; the point of having three is to not be attached to one.

### A. A last small job

Open mid-case on something grubby and beneath her — a skip trace, an insurance
tail, somebody's partner photographed leaving somewhere. She solves it, or
mostly solves it, and it pays badly. Then the Eyeswide letter.

- **For:** establishes voice, competence, the lower levels, and the texture of
  the life she's about to leave. The letter lands as a hook rather than a
  premise. Most noir of the three.
- **Against:** slowest start. Needs a small case that's actually interesting,
  built fresh.

### B. Cold open on Oash

Start in the room. The acceptance interview, or the moment just after it.

- **For:** fastest into the engine. Introduces Oash immediately, and Oash is
  where the campaign's second layer lives.
- **Against:** skips showing who Shan is on her own. Player meets the agency
  before they've met the detective.

### C. Something from the community

Ifset, or someone through Ifset — the pahtra mutual-aid fund has a member who
stopped collecting. Nobody has reported anything. Ifset mentions it the way you
mention an annoyance, while criticising Shan for something else.

- **For:** uses the sanctioned Ifset material, points at revelation A without
  delivering it, and arrives through a relationship rather than a client. Also
  puts the old case in front of her on the same day the letter comes, which is
  a good coincidence because it isn't one — the pipeline never stopped.
- **Against:** risks front-loading the pahtra material the player deprioritised.
  Keep Ifset's screen time short and the needling light.

**Do not play the returned woman** (loose clue: came back eighteen months ago,
found her flat re-let and her name on a voting roll) **in session one.** She is
a direct proof of revelation A and should be earned. Hold her.

## Encounter budget, if a fight happens

Solo, 1st level. From `notes/gm/STATE.md`:

- Moderate = **20 XP** = one level −1 creature.
- Severe = **30 XP** = one level 0 creature.
- Extreme = **40 XP** = **one single level-1 creature.**

She has 16 HP. A first-session fight should be *avoidable*, telegraphed, and
ideally something she chooses from cover with Aim already spent. If a fight
happens at all, one level −1 opponent is plenty.

Hero Point at session start, per GM Core.

## Things to remember while running

- **Report observation, withhold interpretation.** She's a detective; the
  inference is the player's job and the whole reason they're here.
- **Never confirm a theory.** Not by agreeing, not by denying, and not by
  warming up when they're right or going quiet when they're wrong.
- **The characters play it straight.** Nobody in the fiction finds the monologue
  funny. The humour is in the gap.
- **Roll before narrating.** `python3 scripts/roll.py '<expr>'`. Say what's at
  stake before the dice, every time.
- **After every scene: did anyone take a position?** Write it down.
- **Write notes in small pieces as you go**, not as a summary at the end.
