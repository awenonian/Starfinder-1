## What Shan knows about the old case

She has never had evidence for any of this. That is the whole problem.

- Three years ago she caught a missing-person case on a lower habitation level.
  The file said the person left the station voluntarily on a labour contract.
  The signature was forged.
- People from the lower levels — indebted, uninsured, transient — are being
  moved off Absalom Station on forged labour contracts. They go. They don't come
  back. Nothing on the station's records says they've gone.
- It is systematic, not opportunistic.
- She has one name at the clerical end: **Deputy Registrar Hollis Wen**, Office
  of Vital Records. She has never been able to do anything with it.
- It was signed off well above Lieutenant Keshvet's head.

## The world, as Shan understands it

**Absalom Station.** The great hub of the Pact Worlds, built during the Gap with
the Starstone at its core; nobody alive knows who built it or why, and everyone
has agreed to stop finding that disturbing. Millions of people live inside a
structure whose blueprints do not exist.

Stratified vertically, and the whole vocabulary of the place is about level. The
upper rings have windows. The lower levels have pipework and rent, and the
further down you go the more the station stops being a city and starts being
machinery that people happen to live inside. Shan works down there mostly.

**A Drift-lane docking expansion is coming.** Enormous public works, enormous
money. Everyone knows. Nobody has publicly worked out exactly where the value
lands.

**Power** sits with the corporations that hold the leases, the residents'
assemblies that theoretically control them, and Absalom Station Security, which
is under-resourced in precisely the neighbourhoods where that is convenient.

**The Eyeswide Agency** — private detectives who train their people psychically.
"Headscanners," other people call them, and they let it stand. Better funded
than the police and less accountable. They have archives, leverage, and reach.
