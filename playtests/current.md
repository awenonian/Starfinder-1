**Logged by the GM, session zero, at the player's explicit prompting — they
said "that's a bug to patch," which is this file's job even though they didn't
use the `log:` prefix.**

> "Lines and Veils. I think you should have started with some, if not let me
> know, because that's a bug to patch."

**Context.** `.claude/rules/session-zero.md` puts lines and veils at the *start
of step 3*, after step 1 (character, mechanically) and step 2 (motivations,
goals, personality, connections). The stated reasoning is that it's cheapest to
ask "before there is any content to remove."

**The bug.** That reasoning is wrong, because content is generated before step 3
— by the rule's own instructions. Step 2 requires the GM to resolve, privately
and in writing, what is true under each connection *at the moment it lands*.
The GM therefore authors campaign material one full step before the player is
asked what to keep out of it.

That is exactly what happened here. Working from the player's step-2 history,
the GM built and wrote down a full antagonist structure whose central mechanism
brushed one of the two lines the player named a step later (body horror). It had
to be rewritten. No harm done — nothing had reached the table, and the rewrite
was an improvement — but it was luck, not process.

**Suggested patch:** move lines and veils to the top of the procedure, ahead of
step 1, or at minimum ahead of step 2's "your side" resolution work. The
argument for asking early is *stronger* than the rule states, not weaker: the
GM's private authoring starts in step 2, so the question has to precede step 2,
not step 3.
